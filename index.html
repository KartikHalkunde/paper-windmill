Experiment report

Construct a small paper or cardboard windmill, test its rotation under natural wind or fan airflow at different speeds, and document how blade movement demonstrates the conversion of wind energy into mechanical motion

Linking the model to traditional wind-powered systems and modern turbines.

01Aim

To construct a small paper or cardboard windmill, observe its rotation under different wind speeds, and understand how wind energy is converted into mechanical motion.

02Introduction

Wind is a renewable and clean source of energy. For centuries, humans have used wind to do useful work such as grinding grain, pumping water and sailing boats. Today the same principle is used in modern wind turbines to generate electricity.

A windmill works because moving air exerts a force on its blades. That force makes the blades rotate, converting the kinetic energy of the wind into mechanical energy. In a modern wind turbine, this mechanical energy is further converted into electrical energy by a generator.

This simple experiment demonstrates the basic working principle of wind energy conversion.

03Materials required

  • Square sheet of paper or thin cardboard (15 cm × 15 cm)
  • Scissors
  • Scale
  • Pencil
  • Push pin
  • Straw or wooden stick
  • Eraser or cork
  • Adhesive tape
  • Electric fan (optional)

04Procedure

  1. Take a square sheet of paper or cardboard.
  2. Draw diagonal lines from each corner towards the centre.
  3. Cut along each diagonal up to about three-fourths of the distance to the centre.
  4. Fold every alternate corner towards the centre.
  5. Fix the folded corners with a push pin.
  6. Attach the windmill to a straw or wooden stick so that it rotates freely.

Observation setup

The windmill is tested under four airflow conditions while the blade movement is observed carefully: no wind, slow natural wind, medium fan speed and high fan speed.

07Principle

When moving air strikes the blades of the windmill, it applies a force on them, and this force makes the blades rotate.

In the paper windmill

Wind energy (kinetic)→Rotational mechanical energy

In a modern wind turbine, the shaft drives a generator

Wind energy→Mechanical energy→Electrical energy

08Working of the windmill

  1. Wind hits the blades.
  2. The blades begin to rotate.
  3. Rotation turns the central shaft.
  4. Mechanical energy is produced.
  5. In modern turbines, the rotating shaft drives a generator to produce electricity.

09Observations

  • The windmill rotates only when wind is present.
  • As wind speed increases, blade rotation also increases.
  • Larger airflow produces greater mechanical motion.
  • Smooth, balanced blades rotate more efficiently.
  • Wind direction also affects blade rotation.

10Analysis

The experiment clearly shows that moving air contains energy. When the wind strikes the blades, the kinetic energy of the air is transferred to the windmill, causing it to rotate.

The faster the wind blows, the greater the force acting on the blades, so the rotational speed of the windmill increases with wind speed. This simple model demonstrates the same working principle used in large wind turbines.

The experiment also shows that blade shape and blade angle play an important role in capturing wind energy efficiently.

How the simulator estimates RPM

Blade tip speed ≈ 0.6 × (wind speed − 0.5 m/s), and RPM = tip speed × 60 ÷ (2π × 0.075 m). The 0.5 m/s cut-in represents friction at the push pin, and 7.5 cm is the blade radius of a 15 cm sheet.

12Advantages

  • Renewable source of energy
  • Environment-friendly
  • Produces no air pollution
  • Reduces dependence on fossil fuels
  • Low operating cost after installation
  • Suitable for sustainable development

13Limitations

  • Wind is not available continuously
  • Electricity generation depends on wind speed
  • High installation cost
  • Requires large open areas
  • Can produce noise during operation

14Applications

  • Electricity generation
  • Water pumping
  • Agricultural irrigation
  • Rural electrification
  • Battery charging
  • Offshore wind farms
  • Small household wind generators

15Precautions

  • Make sure the blades are equal in size.
  • Fix the push pin loosely so the blades rotate freely.
  • Keep fingers away from fast-moving blades.
  • Perform the experiment in an open area or in front of a fan.
  • Hold the windmill firmly during testing.

16Conclusion

The paper windmill successfully demonstrates the conversion of wind energy into mechanical motion. As the wind speed increases, the blades rotate faster, showing that stronger winds produce more mechanical energy.

The experiment explains the basic working principle of both traditional windmills, used for grinding grain and pumping water, and modern wind turbines that generate electricity. It highlights the importance of wind as a clean, renewable and sustainable source of energy.
